Obviously Awesome

Thesis in short:

  • Market positioning is more important than product quality in regards to selling products. A professional violinist may get $38 in a subway and $10,000 in a concert hall.

Ideas I'll actually use:

  1. The most valuable yet hardest thing to do is building your own category; Thus you completely own market positioning.
  2. Customers will likely default to a spreadsheet, paper and pen, hiring someone or doing nothing if they don't buy your product, not a competitor.
  3. Disregard your original assumptions for market positioning. Always be open to change in this regard. Ask other members of the team.
  4. Good ways to capture customers attention is to work within a space that is known and customers understand, but bring something that differentiates.
  5. If you don't position correctly, the customer will auto-position you, 99% of the time in a box you don't want to be in.
  6. Talk to a lot of people to understand their market position.
