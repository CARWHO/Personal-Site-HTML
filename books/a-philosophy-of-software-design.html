A Philosophy of Software Design

Ideas I'll actually use:

  1. Depth > Length. Depth = What does this module do for me?/ What do I need to know to use it?
  2. Good design is done by emphasizing what you do need, and minimizing what you don't.
  3. Not from this book, just idea: When designing modular code, adapters/ seams should be clear. Seam = where module meets something it needs to be plumbed into (best space for testing). Adapter = required to do conversion at seam.
  4. Interfaces are contracts. E.g. I give you X, you give me Y.
  5. Objects store data and func's related, required to do something.
  6. Classes are blueprints for objects.
  7. Define errors out of existence. Just: else -> Fix
  8. Focus on making reading easy, not writing (make interfaces simple first, then focus on their implementation)
  9. Pull down complexity
