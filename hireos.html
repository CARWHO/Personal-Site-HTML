HireOS

July 2026 to present. hireos.co.nz

A rental management system for equipment hire companies. I built it and sell it on my own. It is live at five companies:

  • Accent Productions
  • Illumination Optics
  • NES Hire
  • TES Hire
  • The Rock Factory

Accent Productions is my dad's company and was the first pilot. It is also the client from the 2024 wind load analysis. The other four came after the pilot proved the product worked.

What it does

A customer fills in a company's public form or rings the company's number. An AI pipeline drafts a proposal from what they said, the RMS turns it into a priced draft, and the company gets an email. From there the company works the booking through quoting, approval, payment, and crewing in an admin workspace, and the numbers land in Xero.

So a job arrives already drafted and priced, and the company's first action is to check it rather than to type it.

Two halves

HireOS is one product in two repos, and the split is the most interesting decision in it.

Intake is the entry half: the public form, the phone line, the AI pipeline, and the email. Everything after the draft is not its concern.

The RMS is the rest: stock, phase, approval, payment, crewing, accounting.

They started as one repo. In August 2026 they became separate repos, separate services, and on 27 August separate databases. The RMS became one Backend behind intake, an interface rather than a fixed thing. A company that already runs its own rental system is a second implementation of that interface, and Rentman is a later one. Such a company still gets intake and an email, and nothing from the HireOS RMS repo at all.

Intake cannot query the RMS's database. The only two ways across are one inbound HTTP interface and one outbound call path.

Capability degradation

A Backend declares what it can answer. When it lacks a capability, the feature that needed it degrades to whatever existed before that read did: known bookings degrade to nothing, booking facts to silence, the estimate is hidden, availability becomes unlimited stock. Nothing breaks, and nothing pretends to know what it does not.

Two sign-ins

Intake has its own sign-in, deliberately not shared with the RMS's table of the same name. Two products, two sign-ins, two databases. Someone who works at a company running both signs into both.

The phone path

Calls run over SIP with a control-only sideband. Tenant resolution for a call is a custom SIP header. An answered call is an intake path, the same as a form submission. A receptionist answers, and a caller-directory match can recognise a number that already belongs to a contractor or supplier.

The rule that holds it together: the transcript proposes, it never writes. Nothing a caller says lands in a booking without a person confirming it.

Vocabulary as a design tool

Before the split, the repo got a context document that defines every term in the domain, with an explicit list of synonyms not to use. Company, not client, tenant, or account. Customer, not client or contact. Contractor, not crew or freelancer. Writing the glossary first, with the banned words, was worth more than it looked. Once the product spanned two repos, two teams of agents, and a Backend interface, having one name per thing meant an ADR written on one side was readable on the other without translation.

The decisions themselves are recorded in architecture decision records, eighteen of them at the time of writing. The split, the Backend interface, the SIP sideband, the header-based tenant resolution, the call-as-intake-path, and the transcript rule are each one record.

Stack

  • Go 1.26, chi, pgx against Postgres, goose migrations
  • Google Cloud: Cloud Tasks, Cloud Storage, Firebase auth, gRPC
  • React with react-router, built with Bun workspaces; Astryx, a StyleX-based design system, with one shared theme across both apps
  • Xero for accounting
  • Admin workspace at workspace.hireos.co.nz, customer and contractor token pages at app.hireos.co.nz

Scale

  • Intake: about 76,000 lines, 474 commits over the same period
